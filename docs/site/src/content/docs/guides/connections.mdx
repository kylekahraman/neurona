`projection(source, target)` describes a connection. The endpoints can be
named cell ports, coordinates, or layer sites. Put the records in a circuit's
`connections` tuple.

## Connect two cells

<Example name="02-connections" />

`pc.axon` means “the axon port of the cell whose ID is `pc`”.
`in.soma-west` addresses the left side of the other soma. Cell IDs must be
unique and cannot contain dots.

## Choose the terminal

| `effect` | Terminal |
| --- | --- |
| `"excitatory"` (default) | Arrowhead |
| `"inhibitory"` | Bar |
| `"unspecified"` | Unmarked line |

`target-gap` leaves clearance before a marked terminal reaches its target.
It uses canvas units and does not move the target port itself.

## Choose a target compartment

All cells expose `soma`, `soma-west`, and `soma-east`. Built-in glyphs also
provide `axon` and `dendrite`. Pyramidal and granule cells add `proximal`,
`apical`, `tuft`, and `tuft-west`; pyramidal cells add `basal` and `ais`.

A trunk port can put a connection inside the drawn arbor. To approach a
pyramidal branch from outside, try `oblique-tip-west`, `oblique-tip-east`,
`basal-tip-west`, or `basal-tip-east` and adjust the route.

## Author the route

Use `via: ((1, -1), (2, -1))` for polyline waypoints, or
`controls: ((1, -2), (2, -2))` for a curve. Choose one routing method per
projection. Waypoints and controls can also be cell ports or layer sites.

Routes currently follow exactly the path you author. They do not avoid other
cells or branches automatically. Inspect the result at its intended size,
then adjust the waypoints, cell spacing, or target port.

For a shared stem that branches, add a `junction` to the circuit and connect
individual projections through its `center` port. An ordinary crossing has no
junction or synapse unless you explicitly draw one.

See [Circuits API](/neurona/reference/circuits/) for edge labels, weights,
population boxes, and junctions.
